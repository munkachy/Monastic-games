The export is the pattern (or the chain) as one seamless loop, ready for Remixlive. With Mute on, the drums are left out, so you can export just the rest to put over a beat.

Pick a psalm and verse and its words go into the box, a line for each half-verse (each new line is a breath in the rap); or type your own. ⬆ adds your Grail file (the psalter pack Selah takes); it stays in this browser on this device and is never sent anywhere. Tone variation at 0 is a dead flat robot monotone. On sounds 1–8 the voice plays up the scale; on 9–16 it is chopped, one syllable per key. Rap it on the beat puts one syllable on each key of this sound and writes them into the pattern in the flow you choose, carrying on into the next patterns (chained) if they don't fit in one bar. Stressed syllables (shown in capitals) are put on the beat; a comma rests to the next beat and a full stop or new line to the next half bar (old school: the next bar). Change the flow, or tap 🎲 New flow, and the same rap is laid out again without speaking it again. Cut: Natural speaks the line as a whole and cuts it at the quiet dips; Robot-precise speaks each syllable on its own, so every cut is clean but it sounds more clipped. The voice engine is eSpeak NG from Vox Machina (about 18 MB the first time). Synthetic voice; no human voice recordings used.

The screen holds what you need while playing. ☰ Menu holds the rest: Sounds (load a sound onto the current key), Settings for that sound, Beat and tempo, Voice, your Buttons, Help and the Log.

    
The blue buttons are yours to set (Menu › Buttons): with the Pads sideways, twenty of them, full size, on the left; upright, four beside Menu and eight under the keys. They do: Mute drums, Export, Roll, Beat, Clear, Key wheel, Next key, Oct − / Oct +, 🤖 Voice (opens the Voice page), 🎤 Rap (raps the words typed there onto the current sound), 🎲 Flow (a new flow for that rap), or any FX. The octave buttons move the keys you play on the current sound up or down an octave (two at most; the screen shows OCT+1), and notes you record keep the octave they were played in.

    
Chain (a blue button, or Menu › Rhythm): tap it, tap patterns in the order they should play, tap it again. Tap it twice without choosing any to unchain. Choosing a pattern while chained only shows it: the chain plays on.

    
Clear (a blue button) clears the pattern; with Sound on, it clears the chosen sound instead: off its key, and out of the patterns. ↶ Undo brings either back. ■ Stop also stops every loop that is running.

    
Loops (Menu › Sounds › Loops): drum loops, breakbeats, vinyl and ambience, one loop per key, in Loop mode. While the beat plays, a loop comes in at the next bar, in step, and follows the tempo and key; it stops at the next bar when tapped again, and every loop stops with the beat. Songs (Packs › Songs): songs written for the app from its own sounds, ready to play, chained.

    
Copy / Paste copy the pattern, or in Sound mode the sound: Sound + key 1, Copy, Sound + key 5, Paste.

    
Beats played by drummers (Menu › Rhythm › Drum beat, the second group): 56 beats in 17 styles (hip hop, funk, soul, rock, pop, punk, jazz, Latin, Afrobeat, Afro-Cuban, reggae, New Orleans, blues, dance, country, gospel, highlife), each a bar of a real performance, every hit at the drummer's own timing and loudness, put on the kit you have chosen. 🎲 gives another drummer's beat in the same style. The first group, Generated by the app, are patterns the app makes for each style.

    
Bass and basslines (Menu › Rhythm, under the drum beat): Bass puts a bass sound on sound 1 (808, upright, electric, contrabass, sub, wobble, growl, Reese, saw, square, FM, acid). Bassline chooses a bar of bass to go with the beat. The first groups were played by bassists: 72 bars from real jazz performances, sorted by the chord under them (minor, dominant 7th, major), every note as the bassist played it, moved into your key. They are walking lines, because no hip-hop or funk basslines played by people are free to use. The last group, Generated by the app, are simple lines the app makes (roots on the kicks, boom bap, root and fifth, octaves, long 808 roots, funk sixteenths). ▶ Hear it plays the line with your beat; Put this bassline in writes it on sound 1 in the current pattern, replacing only sound 1's notes (↶ Undo takes it back); 🎲 Another picks another line of the same kind.

    
Beats: pick a style in the Rhythm tab and put its beat into the current pattern, or press 🎲 Beat for a new one in that style, as often as you like. It plays on the drum kit you have chosen (or the first kit on the keys), and replaces only the drums already there: your bass and other sounds stay. ↶ Undo takes back any change, one at a time.

    
If the sound crackles on an older iPad: Menu › Buttons › Sound engine › Light. The app notices when the sound keeps breaking up and suggests it. Light gives the device more time to make each moment of sound (a little more delay between tap and sound), works at a lower rate inside, uses less memory, has a shorter reverb and plays fewer notes at once. Sounds only tried in Menu › Sounds are let go when the menu closes, so they don't fill the memory.

    
Sideways: the Pads lie sideways, like the Loops: the screen and buttons on the left, the keys on the right. Held upright, the whole app is turned a quarter turn, so it reads the right way up with the phone held sideways (turn the phone to the left); if the phone really turns, it simply lies flat. Menu › Buttons › Pads › Upright brings back the tall layout.

    
Pads and Loops (the two tabs at the top): Pads is the sampler; Loops is a grid like Remixlive's, always laid out sideways (held upright, it is turned so it reads right with the phone held sideways). Eight columns in their parts' colours (Kick, Snare, Tops, Bass, Chords, Lead, Sub, Vox/Keys), six cells each. Tap a cell and it starts on the next bar, in time and in key, and ▶ Play starts if it wasn't playing. One cell plays in a column: tap another and it takes over on the bar; tap the playing one and it stops on the bar; tap a waiting (blinking) one to take it back. The ring shows how far through its loop it is. Hold a cell (or use ✎ Edit) for its settings, in three tabs (Settings, Wave, EQ & Effect): Loop, One shot or Gate (only while held, and always free: at once, no quantize); its own Quantize (or As all: the default for every loop, in ⚙); its own volume; an EQ (low, middle and high, ±12 dB, with its curve); Transpose in half steps; Repeat every 1, 2, 4, 6, 8, 16 or 32 beats (the sound plays again every so many beats: a short one waits, silent, for the beat to come round; a longer one is cut there); Time stretch on (it keeps its pitch at any tempo) or off (it speeds up and slows down with the tempo, like a record); Follow the key (on for every loop, ♪ on the cell; its key is found by listening, so turn it off for a drum loop that sounds wrong moved); an Effect (low pass, high pass, reverb, echo, ping-pong, drive or tape, with an amount) that goes on the whole column, which any loop can be set to skip; its sound, from the loops and packs; and its wave: drag across it to choose the part that plays (it snaps to 16th notes on a loop with a tempo), tap it to hear from there, ▶ Hear it plays the part chosen. ⚙ holds the settings for all the loops: Quantize for all the loops (when a tap lands: the next beat, bar, 2 bars or 4 bars, or Off: at once, still in time; a loop can have its own), Fade in and out, and Stop all loops (on the next point, the Pads beat playing on). Under each column: its volume (drag) and M to mute it. Tempo, key, Play and the effects are the same in both tabs, and ■ Stop stops everything. Whole beats go in Kick (the red column); Snare and Tops hold single hits (a snare, a clap, a hat, a cowbell), which play on the next 16th note, and again on every tap (at once while stopped, without starting the beat). Sets (in ⚙) fill the whole grid at once, at the tempo and key they were made in. Electro Boom Bap: F minor, 92 BPM, swung; six hip-hop beats played by drummers, on the Boom Bap, TR-808 and electro kits; snare and top hits; 808, electric, Reese and upright bass; Rhodes, pad, choir and organ chords; Juno stabs, pluck, chip, trumpet and bell leads; sub bass; the robot's words, the women's choir, vinyl, Alleluia and Selah. Every loop in it fits every other: the harmony runs Fm7, D♭maj7, B♭m7, Cm7, a bar each. The first set: Human Grooves (beats played by drummers) and the Amen, kit hits, and hype words. The set's loops are in Menu › Sounds › Loops too, for the pads.

    
Colours go by part, the same everywhere, in the Pads and the Loops: a kick is always red, a snare orange, hi-hats yellow, a bass green. Sounds 1–8 are the instruments, one part each: 1 Bass (green), 2 Sub (purple), 3 Chords (teal), 4 Lead (pink), 5 Vox/Keys (blue), 6 Pad (indigo), 7 Pluck/Arp (lavender), 8 Stab/FX (magenta), each starting with a sound for its part. Sounds 9–16 are the drums: drum kits and percussion, silver in Sound mode. Inside a kit every hit has its own colour: kick red, snare orange, clap amber, hats yellow, open hat lime, toms coral, percussion mint, cymbals cyan. Kits and percussion load only onto the drums, and instruments only onto 1–8, so the two never mix.

    
Write shows the side of the sound chosen. With a kit chosen: that kit's hits, a block for each part hit on the step, in the hit's colour; a small light shows another kit plays there too. With an instrument chosen: the instruments, a block for each, in its colour (a small light: drums under it). One fills the step, two to four split it side by side, five or more make a small grid. The step now playing has an orange ring. Menu › Buttons › Look brings back the classic beige look.

    
The tags say what a sound is: SYN synthesized as you play (the app's synths: filter, wobble and length really change it); REC a recording, a sample of a real instrument or machine (the TR-808 is the real analog drum machine, recorded); GEN made in code beforehand, then played as a recording (the 808 and 909 kits drawn in code, rolled sounds, vinyl and risers, the robot voice, the loops written for the app); MINE yours. They are in Menu › Sounds, on the pads in Sound mode, on the screen and on the Loops cells.

    
Play: tap the keys. Sounds 1–8 are melodic (the keys go up the scale). Sounds 9–16 are kits and chops (each key is one hit).

    
Sound: tap once for the instruments (🎹 Instr.: sounds 1–8 light up; tap one), again for the drums (🥁 Drums: the kits, 9–16; tap one, and its 16 hits are the keys, each in its colour), again to play. Hold a sound there (an instrument or a kit) for its own menu: its volume, pitch and how it plays, and its own EQ (low, middle, high, with its curve) and effect (reverb, echo, ping-pong, chorus, drive, fuzz or tape, with an amount), on every note of it, in the beat and in the export. Pattern + key: choose one of 16 patterns.

    
Write: tap once for Write, twice for Replace, three times for off. In Replace, playing over a part replaces it: from your first note, the sound's old notes go as the playhead passes and yours go in; if you don't play, nothing is deleted, and it stops replacing once a whole pattern passes without a note. With either on, stopped: the 16 keys are the 16 steps. Play a note first, then tap steps to place it; tap again to remove it. Playing: whatever you play is recorded into the beat, on the nearest step; turn Quantize off in the Rhythm tab to keep it exactly where you played it.

    
FX: tap a key to turn an effect on, tap again to turn it off (or hold, if you switch that in Menu › Effects, where you also choose the effect on each key, from 26: loops, rolls, scratch, pitch, reverb, echo, ping-pong, chorus, filters, crush, overdrive, fuzz, wavefold and tape saturation). Loops, rolls, scratch, half time, scramble, pitch dive, octaves, reverse, chorus, crush, low pass, reverb and ping-pong (an echo bouncing between your ears). In FX mode the screen shows Tracks (tap to choose which sounds the FX keys hit) and the amount of the last effect you touched. With Write on while playing, the effect is saved on every step you hold it through. "Clear effects" takes them out again.

    
Locks: with Write on while playing, moving a sound slider saves that setting on the step that is playing, for the current sound only, as the PO-33 does with its knobs.

    
● Rec: hold it and sing, speak, or beatbox into the phone. Let go and it lands on the current sound, chopped if it's 9–16. While holding Rec, tap a key to record into that sound instead. Set "Rec from" to the beat to resample what's playing.

    
Play as (Sound tab): One shot plays the whole sound however short the tap; Gate plays only while your finger is down; Loop starts repeating the sound, or the slice, when you tap it and keeps going until you tap that key again (the key stays lit). Switch the sound to One shot or Gate and its loops keep going, so you can play over them; come back to Loop and tap a looping key to stop it. In the beat, Gate and Loop notes last as long as they sounded when you recorded them live, or one step if you tapped them in.

    
✂ Editor (Settings › Edit this sound, or after an import): select part of a sound, crop it or cut it out, place the cut points that split it across keys 9–16, then put it on any sound or save it to My Sounds. Instruments, kits and synths open as a recording of the last note played.

    
Menu › Sounds is in folders by part, in the parts' colours: Beats (red: whole beats: Human Grooves played by drummers, the Electro Boom Bap beats, drum loops, breakbeats), Snares and claps (orange: every one of the kits', one by one), Tops (yellow: hats, cymbals, percussion, one by one), Drum kits, Bass (green), Chords (teal), Lead (pink), Sub (purple), Vox and keys (blue), Pads, Plucks, mallets and bells, Stabs and FX, ambience, songs and yours.

    
Favorites and My Sounds (Menu › Sounds): choose any sound, then ☆ Favorite (under Load) keeps it in ★ Favorites, and ★ Save to My Sounds keeps a copy of your own. My Sounds: your saved sounds, ⬆ Import (WAV, MP3, or a screen recording: record with the iPhone's Screen Recording, then pick the video, and its sound opens in the editor), and Make a pack from up to 16 of them. Any pack can be opened to load one of its sounds alone.

    
Key: tap the key on the screen (C MIN) for the key wheel, over the keys. Majors are round the outside, minors inside, in circle-of-fifths order; the shaded keys sit well with the one you're in. Tap any key, or the Next button in the middle (up a fifth, then across to the relative key, by turns); 🎲 picks one of the shaded ones. While the beat plays, the change waits for the top of the next bar (the key blinks until then). With Write on, the key is saved in the pattern, and that pattern always plays in it (the key box is shaded); with Write off, the key changes for now and patterns keep their saved keys. "Forget pattern key" takes it out.

    
Its key (Settings, for recordings and imports): say what key a sound is in, or tap 🔍 to have it found by listening, and it moves to the song's key (never more than six semitones). Left on "Plays as recorded", drums and other recordings stay as they are.

    
Trim: Start and Length cut the sound down. On 9–16 they trim only the slice you played last.

    
🎲 Roll: a brand-new video-game sound on the last key you played ("Roll all 16" in the Sound tab fills a whole kit). 🎲 Beat: a new drum beat in the style chosen in the Rhythm tab.

    
1 Shot / Gate / Loop (the button next to Rec) switches how the current sound's keys play; it is the same as "Play as" in the Sound tab.

    
On the screen, tap the waveform to hear that slice; drag a marker to move where it is cut.

    
Keyboard: 1234 / QWER / ASDF / ZXCV are the keys, space is play.
