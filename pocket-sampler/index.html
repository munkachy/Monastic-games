The screen holds what you need while playing. ☰ Menu holds the rest: Sounds (load a sound onto the current key), Settings for that sound, Beat and tempo, Voice, your Buttons, Help and the Log.

    
The buttons are in rows, a colour for each kind of thing. At the top: Sound, Pattern, FX and ☰ Menu (what the keys do); green, playing and recording (▶ Play, ● Rec, Write, 1 Shot); grey, Copy, Paste, Undo, Redo. The rest are yours to set (Menu › Buttons), and each takes the colour of what it does. At first: red, rhythm (Mute drums, 🎲 Vary, Chain, Rhythm: the Rhythm page); purple, pitch and key (Oct −, Oct +, Key, Key ▸); amber, the sound (🎲 Roll, ✂ Edit, Clear, ⬆ Export); blue, the voice (🎤 Rap, 🎲 Flow, ▶ Rap, 🤖 Voice); pink, effects (reverb, echo, low pass, crush). With the Pads sideways all twenty are on the left, full size; upright, four sit beside Copy and Undo and eight under the keys. They can do: Mute drums, Export, Roll, Beat, Clear, Key wheel, Next key, Oct − / Oct +, 🤖 Voice (opens the Voice page), 🎤 Rap (raps the words typed there onto the current sound), 🎲 Flow (a new flow for that rap), or any FX. The octave buttons move the keys you play on the current sound up or down an octave (two at most; the screen shows OCT+1), and notes you record keep the octave they were played in.

    
Chain (a red button, or Menu › Rhythm): tap it, tap patterns in the order they should play, tap it again. Tap it twice without choosing any to unchain. Choosing a pattern while chained only shows it: the chain plays on.

    
Clear (a blue button) clears the pattern; with Sound on, it clears the chosen sound instead: off its key, and out of the patterns. ↶ Undo brings either back. ■ Stop also stops every loop that is running.

    
Loops (Menu › Sounds › Loops): drum loops, breakbeats, old records and ambience, one loop per key, in Loop mode. While the beat plays, a loop comes in at the next bar, in step, and follows the tempo and key; it stops at the next bar when tapped again, and every loop stops with the beat. Songs (Packs › Songs): songs written for the app from its own sounds, ready to play, chained.

    
Copy / Paste copy the pattern, or in Sound mode the sound: Sound + key 1, Copy, Sound + key 5, Paste.

    
Beats played by drummers (Menu › Rhythm › Drum beat, the second group): 56 beats in 17 styles (hip hop, funk, soul, rock, pop, punk, jazz, Latin, Afrobeat, Afro-Cuban, reggae, New Orleans, blues, dance, country, gospel, highlife), each a bar of a real performance, every hit at the drummer's own timing and loudness, put on the kit you have chosen. 🎲 gives another drummer's beat in the same style. The first group, Generated by the app, are patterns the app makes for each style.

    
🤖 Robot (a blue button): type words on the pads. Tap a pad's box and type a word (or a few); the robot voice says it (the voice and cut chosen on the Voice page) and cuts it into syllables, all on that pad at first. − and + choose how many syllables stay on the pad; the rest spill over onto the next pad (shown dimmer), which has its own − and +, so they can spill on again. A word typed on a spilled pad takes its place. ⚙ on a pad gives its word a voice of its own (the voice, pitch, tone variation, speed, word gap, vowel length and cut, as on the Voice page), so one pattern can hold many voices; the ⚙ lights when a word has its own. On a spilled pad, ⚙ is the voice of the word it came from. If syllables don't fit (the next pad has a word of its own, or the pads run out), the line under the buttons says so. Tap a pad, not its box, to hear it. The button is in the blue row with the Pads sideways; upright, put 🤖 Robot on one of the first twelve buttons (Menu › Buttons). The pads are the keys of one sound (the Robot pads, on a free sound, never a drum kit), always at the voice's normal pitch (whatever the tempo, the key, the octave buttons or the Voice page's pitch), so they record, write, take effects and export like any other sound; ↶ Undo takes back any change. 🤖 Done goes back to playing; the Voice page is still in the Menu.

    
Drums never change key. When the key changes, the bass and every other instrument move to it, but the drums (the kits, hand drums, a single drum hit on a key, drum loops, and the Kick, Snare and Tops columns of the Loops) play exactly as they are, in any key.

    
Bass and basslines (Menu › Rhythm, under the drum beat): Bass puts a bass sound on sound 1 (808, upright, electric, contrabass, sub, wobble, growl, Reese, saw, square, FM, acid). Bassline chooses a bar of bass to go with the beat. The first groups were played by bassists: 72 bars from real jazz performances, sorted by the chord under them (minor, dominant 7th, major), every note as the bassist played it, moved into your key. They are walking lines, because no hip-hop or funk basslines played by people are free to use. The last group, Generated by the app, are simple lines the app makes (roots on the kicks, boom bap, root and fifth, octaves, long 808 roots, funk sixteenths). ▶ Hear it plays the line with your beat; Put this bassline in writes it on sound 1 in the current pattern, replacing only sound 1's notes (↶ Undo takes it back); 🎲 Another picks another line of the same kind.

    
Beats: pick a style in the Rhythm tab and put its beat into the current pattern, or press 🎲 Make a new one there for a new one in that style, as often as you like. It plays on the drum kit you have chosen (or the first kit on the keys), and replaces only the drums already there: your bass and other sounds stay. ↶ Undo takes back any change, one at a time.

    
If the sound crackles on an older iPad: Menu › Buttons › Sound engine › Light. The app notices when the sound keeps breaking up and suggests it. Light gives the device more time to make each moment of sound (a little more delay between tap and sound), works at a lower rate inside, uses less memory, has a shorter reverb and plays fewer notes at once. Sounds only tried in Menu › Sounds are let go when the menu closes, so they don't fill the memory.

    
Sideways: the Pads lie sideways, like the Loops: the screen and buttons on the left, the keys on the right. Held upright, the whole app is turned a quarter turn, so it reads the right way up with the phone held sideways (turn the phone to the left); if the phone really turns, it simply lies flat. Menu › Buttons › Pads › Upright brings back the tall layout.

    
Loops + Pads (the third tab at the top, where the app opens): the loops on the left, four columns a page (◀ ▶ flips to the other four), the 16 pads on the right, held sideways. The pads play the loop you tapped last (a drum loop too; a white dot marks its cell), in its colour, in one of two ways: Slices, the loop cut into 16 equal parts, one a pad (a one-bar loop: a 16th note each), or Notes, its first note, played down and up the key across the pads (the 8th pad, marked •, is the note as played; a melody's first note is moved to the key's root first, a drum's is left as it is). The pads here only play: to write patterns or choose their sounds, go to Pads.

    
Loops, Pads and Loops + Pads (the three tabs at the top): Pads is the sampler; Loops is a grid like Remixlive's, always laid out sideways (held upright, it is turned so it reads right with the phone held sideways). Eight columns in their parts' colours (Kick, Snare, Tops, Bass, Chords, Lead, Sub/Color, Vox/Keys), six cells each. Tap a cell and it starts on the next bar, in time and in key, and ▶ Play starts if it wasn't playing. One cell plays in a column: tap another and it takes over on the bar; tap the playing one and it stops on the bar; tap a waiting (blinking) one to take it back. The ring shows how far through its loop it is (a One shot or a Gate shows a straight line instead, filling as it plays). Hold a cell (or use ✎ Edit) for its settings. In ✎ Edit the grid turns shades of amber, so you can see you are editing, and you can drag a cell to another place: onto an empty cell it moves, onto a full one the two swap (in its own column a playing loop plays on; moved to another column it stops). 🗑 Clear all (there only while ✎ Edit is on) empties every cell and stops the loops, for a fresh start; the sounds stay in the library, and ↶ Undo clear brings the grid back until you leave ✎ Edit. (To keep a grid first: Loops › ⚙ › 💾 Save the grid as a set.) Its settings, in two tabs (Settings, with the wave at the top, and EQ & Effect): Loop, One shot or Gate (only while held, and always free: at once, no quantize); its own Quantize (1/32, 1/16, 1/8, 1/4, 1/2 note, 1, 2 or 4 bars, Off, or As all: the default for every loop, in ⚙) (Off or under a bar, a tapped loop starts from its beginning right away, on that point, and repeats from the moment you tapped; Write shows the side of the sound chosen. With a kit chosen: that kit's hits, a block for each part hit on the step, in the hit's colour; a small light shows another kit plays there too. With an instrument chosen: the instruments, a block for each, in its colour (a small light: drums under it). One fills the step, two to four split it side by side, five or more make a small grid. The tags say what a sound is: GEN made in code and kept as a recording (the synths, the 808, 909, CR-78, 606 and Minimal kits, the modelled guitars, the loops the app played: every one is played from a WAV, which is far easier on the phone than making the sound for each note); REC a recording, a sample of a real instrument or machine (the TR-808 is the real analog drum machine, recorded); GEN made in code beforehand, then played as a recording (the 808 and 909 kits drawn in code, rolled sounds, rain and risers, the robot voice, the loops written for the app); MINE yours (recorded, imported, designed in Menu › Design). They are in Menu › Sounds, on the pads in Sound mode, on the screen and on the Loops cells.

    
Play: tap the keys. Sounds 1–8 are melodic (the keys go up the scale). Sounds 9–16 are kits and chops (each key is one hit).

    
Sound: tap once for the instruments (🎹 Instr.: sounds 1–8 light up; tap one), again for the drums (🥁 Drums: the kits, 9–16; tap one, and its 16 hits are the keys, each in its colour), again to play. Hold a sound there (an instrument or a kit) for its own menu: its volume, pitch and how it plays, and its own EQ (low, middle, high, with its curve) and effect (reverb, echo, ping-pong, chorus, drive, fuzz or tape, with an amount), on every note of it, in the beat and in the export. Pattern + key: choose one of 16 patterns.

    
Write: tap once for Write, twice for Replace, three times for off. In Replace, playing over a part replaces it: from your first note, the sound's old notes go as the playhead passes and yours go in; if you don't play, nothing is deleted, and it stops replacing once a whole pattern passes without a note. With either on, stopped: the 16 keys are the 16 steps. Play a note first, then tap steps to place it; tap again to remove it. Playing: whatever you play is recorded into the beat, on the nearest step; turn Quantize off in the Rhythm tab to keep it exactly where you played it.

    
FX: tap a key to turn an effect on, tap again to turn it off (or hold, if you switch that in Menu › Effects, where you also choose the effect on each key, from 26: loops, rolls, scratch, pitch, reverb, echo, ping-pong, chorus, filters, crush, overdrive, fuzz, wavefold and tape saturation). Loops, rolls, scratch, half time, scramble, pitch dive, octaves, reverse, chorus, crush, low pass, reverb and ping-pong (an echo bouncing between your ears). In FX mode the screen shows Tracks (tap to choose which sounds the FX keys hit) and the amount of the last effect you touched. With Write on while playing, the effect is saved on every step you hold it through. "Clear effects" takes them out again.

    
Locks: with Write on while playing, moving a sound slider saves that setting on the step that is playing, for the current sound only, as the PO-33 does with its knobs.

    
● Rec: hold it and sing, speak, or beatbox into the phone. Let go and it lands on the current sound, chopped if it's 9–16. While holding Rec, tap a key to record into that sound instead. Set "Rec from" to the beat to resample what's playing.

    
Play as (Sound tab): One shot plays the whole sound however short the tap; Gate plays only while your finger is down; Loop starts repeating the sound, or the slice, when you tap it and keeps going until you tap that key again (the key stays lit). Switch the sound to One shot or Gate and its loops keep going, so you can play over them; come back to Loop and tap a looping key to stop it. In the beat, Gate and Loop notes last as long as they sounded when you recorded them live, or one step if you tapped them in.

    
✂ Editor (Settings › Edit this sound, or after an import): select part of a sound, crop it or cut it out, place the cut points that split it across keys 9–16, then put it on any sound or save it to My Sounds. Instruments, kits and synths open as a recording of the last note played.

    
⏺ Loop rec (an amber button, ⏺ Loop): record what you play over the beat or the Loops, straight into a loop. Choose how: Notes (your taps on the pads, on the beat as Quantize puts them; afterwards ▶ Hear it, ✓ Keep it, or ✎ Fix it as patterns: the notes go into empty patterns, chained, to fix a note before ⟳ Make a loop) or Sound (the sound itself, like a looper pedal: the pads alone, without the Loops grid or the effects on the whole mix, or everything you hear); how many bars (1, 2, 4 or 8); and the Loops column it goes in (or a guess from what you play). Start: it begins on the next bar (if nothing is playing, Play starts and a bar counts in), shows the bar it is on, and stops by itself; tap it again to stop early. Kept, it goes in the first empty cell of its column (the next column with room, if that one is full), playing from the next bar, and in the library (My Sounds, its folder, My loops), in time at any tempo.

    
Design (Menu › Design): make your own sounds. 🥁 Drum hit: a kick, snare, clap, hat, open hat or ride, tom, percussion (wood, metal or glass: a woodblock, rimshot, cowbell, chime) or shaker, each a model of how that drum makes its sound, with its own knobs (a kick's pitch, how far and fast it drops, its length, click, beater, knock, sub; a snare's tuning, body and wires; drive, tone and Old sampler, a 1980s sampler's grit, on most). Start from one of its starting points, turn the knobs, ▶ Hear it; 🎲 Surprise makes a new one near a starting point, ↻ New strike the same with different noise. Each pitch shows its note (52 Hz A♭1), and ♪ In key tunes the drum to the nearest note of the song's key. Finish, for every drum: Soften the hit (less click), Low cut and High cut (so a layer keeps to its own range), Layer (a second drum under it, from any starting point, lined up so they never flam, at Layer level), Room (a short real room, baked in) and Reverse. ＋ Add puts it in a kit of yours (up to 16 hits; New kit makes one), which loads on a drum sound (9–16) from Menu › Sounds › My packs; ★ Save keeps it alone in My Sounds. 🎹 Synth: two waves (sine, triangle, saw, square; the second tuned and detuned), a sub an octave down, noise, unison voices; a filter (low, band or high pass) with resonance and an envelope that opens it; the volume's attack, decay, sustain and release; vibrato, a glide into each note, and drive. Start from Warm pad, Analog bass, Pluck, Supersaw lead, Howl, Bell keys or Organ, in a Bass, Middle or High range. ▶ Hear it plays one note, tapped then held; Put on sound records the whole instrument (a note every 6 half steps, tapped and held, a seamless loop for held notes) onto that sound, to play and record into patterns and loops; ★ Save keeps it in My Sounds. Every sound made here is made once and kept as a recording.

    
🥁 Design in the Loops: hold a cell for its settings, and its third tab, 🥁 Design, makes a hit and puts it straight in: tap the steps of one bar (on, soft, off; the column suggests a start: kicks on 1 and 3, snares on 2 and 4, hats in eighths), make the hit below (the same designer as Menu › Design), ＋ Add a line for another drum on its own steps (up to 6 lines: kick, snare, hat, clap and so on; tap a line's name to shape that one's drum, ✕ takes it away), ▶ Hear the bar, and ✓ Put in this cell: a one-bar loop at the song's tempo and swing, playing from the next bar, also kept in the library (My loops). An empty cell has 🥁 Design a hit at the top of its picker.

    
🎹 From the pads (at the top of a cell's picker: an empty cell, or Change the sound): what is on the Pads now, the pattern or the whole chain as one long loop, as you hear it (a muted beat stays out), made into a loop at the song's tempo and put in the cell, playing from the next bar. It is kept in the library too (My loops), so it is there after a reload.

    
Basic and Advanced (at the top of Menu › Design and of a cell's 🥁 Design): Basic shows the few knobs that shape each sound most (a kick's pitch, pitch drop, length, click and drive; a snare's tune, body, wires and snap; a synth's wave, sub, cutoff, resonance, attack, release, vibrato and drive) and the Room; Advanced shows every knob, the layer, and the whole finish (soften, low and high cut, reverse). The choice holds for every designer on this device, and what you set in Advanced stays when you go back to Basic.

    
Sets and pack files (Loops › ⚙): 💾 Save the grid as a set keeps the whole grid (every cell and its settings, the column effects, tempo, key and reverb) in My sets, to load again in one tap (hold one to share, rename or delete it). ⬆ Share as a pack file makes one file with the grid and every sound of yours in it, to keep in Files or iCloud Drive; ⬇ Open a pack file (or My Sounds › ⬆ Import) opens one on any device: its sounds go into My Sounds and its set into My sets. My Sounds › ⬆ Share as a pack file puts all your sounds in one file (a backup, too).

Key: tap the key on the screen (C MIN) for the key wheel, over the keys. Majors are round the outside, minors inside, in circle-of-fifths order; the shaded keys sit well with the one you're in. Tap any key, or the Next button in the middle (up a fifth, then across to the relative key, by turns); 🎲 picks one of the shaded ones. While the beat plays, the change waits for the top of the next bar (the key blinks until then). With Write on, the key is saved in the pattern, and that pattern always plays in it (the key box is shaded); with Write off, the key changes for now and patterns keep their saved keys. "Forget pattern key" takes it out.

    
Its key (Settings, for recordings and imports): say what key a sound is in, or tap 🔍 to have it found by listening, and it moves to the song's key (never more than six semitones). Left on "Plays as recorded", drums and other recordings stay as they are.

    
Trim: Start and Length cut the sound down. On 9–16 they trim only the slice you played last.

    
🎲 Roll: a brand-new video-game sound on the last key you played ("Roll all 16" in the Sound tab fills a whole kit). 🎲 Vary: the beat you already have, with one or two small changes a drummer might make: a kick pushed onto an off-beat or played early, ghost notes on the snare, an open hat, a quick double on the hi-hat, a pickup or a little fill at the end of the bar, a kick left out for space, the hat played with accents. The screen says what changed. Each press starts again from the same beat, so it never wanders off into another one; change the beat by hand (or put a new one in) and that becomes the beat it varies. With no beat in the pattern, it puts one in. 🎲 New (you can put it on a button) makes a new beat in the style chosen on the Rhythm page.
